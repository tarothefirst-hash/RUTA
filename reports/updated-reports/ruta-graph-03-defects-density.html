Two accepted risks, recorded rather than unnoticed

Both are recorded in docs/ACCEPTED-RISKS.md and accepted for this capstone. Accepted means known and shipped with deliberately: it does not mean safe. Neither has been tested or exploited, and both fixes are proposed, not implemented.

Accepted riskWorst caseProposed fix
The matched-booking window One rule clause keys only on the booking being in its matching window, so a modified app could take over such a booking, and a driver whose approval is not granted could claim one. Move the matching step into a server-side matching function that performs the cross-booking update with admin rights.
Six database nodes open to any signed-in user Trusted contacts, pair requests, emergency alerts, chats, trip groups and match locks stay open because those flows cross users. The worst case is injecting into another user's trusted contacts and then receiving their SOS with live location. Scope each node to the parties involved, then retest pairing and alerts.

What has not been proven

  • Nothing has been run on a device or emulator. An attempt is impossible on this machine: the only emulator profile points at an empty system image, and no hypervisor driver is installed. Every figure here is a static review of the final tree.
  • The database rules have never been executed. They are written and indexed, not deployed and not tested.
  • The three partial classes above cannot be closed from code alone: they need a deploy, an enabled Storage service and scoped queries.
  • The minified release build has never been run, because it is unsigned and cannot be installed as produced.
